#Fire ecology in Texas ecosystems
Research on how fire (wildfire and prescribed fire) affects ecosystems that occur in Texas, including longleaf pine, shortleaf pine, ponderosa pine, pinyon-oak-juniper, short-grass prairie, tallgrass prairie, coastal prairie, oak shrublands, oak savannas, Ashe-juniper woodlands, desert cienegas, pitcher plant bogs, bottomland hardwood forests, and Tamaulipan thornscrub. I am interested in papers with strong implications for land managers and papers with new and innovative ways to measure fire impacts and ecosystem responses. I am especially interested in papers that use long-term datasets to evaluate ecosystem responses to multiple fires. I am also interested in seeing new ways to use drones/UAVs/UAS to measure fire impacts. Research about pre-settlement fire regimes and how climate change may influence these ecosystems going forward is also useful. Major papers examining floristic quality, especially those that publish lists of coefficients of conservatism, can be included.

#Pyrodiversity
Research on pyrodiversity, especially in frequent-fire systems like grasslands and savannas and for projects that examine repeated fires. I am interested in reviews of the literature as well as papers that describe new approaches to pyrodiversity.

#Rare species found in Texas
Research on rare plants and animals of Texas, especially black-capped vireos (Vireo atricapilla), golden-cheeked warblers (Dendroica chrysoparia), Attwater's prairie chickens, Texas snowbells (Styrax platanifolius ssp. texas), puzzle/Pecos sunflower (Helianthus paradoxus), Leoncita false foxglove (Agalinis calycina), and Tobusch fishhook cactus (Sclerocactus brevihamatus ssp. tobuschii), as well as closely related species. I am interested in basic life history, habitat management, population estimates and trends, and habitat or population modeling. For the birds, I am also interested in research on how urban/suburban development affects productivity and behavior, as well as approaches using acoustic monitoring (AudioMoths) that are relevant to these species.

#Soil carbon responses to grassland management, including grazing and prescribed fire
Research on how grazing and prescribed fire affect soil carbon. I am particularly interested in Texas studies that are well-designed and replicated. Studies that track long-term soil carbon trends using repeated sampling and repeated management (especially fire), and studies that examine the effects of invasive grasses and brush/shrub/woody plant encroachment are also useful. Studies that include additional factors that explain soil carbon (like rainfall, soil texture, drought, etc.) are also useful.

#Management of grasslands and savannas
Research on how to manage brush/shrub/woody plant encroachment in Texas grassland and savanna ecosystems using prescribed fire, mechanical treatments, and chemical treatments. Focal ecosystems include short-grass prairie, tallgrass prairie, coastal prairie, longleaf pine, and oak savannas; research doesn't have to be done in Texas but should be relevant to these Texas ecosystems. Native encroaching species can include mesquite, Ashe juniper, yaupon, oaks (in longleaf pine), huisache, Baccharis, and more. Also include non-native woody plants like Chinese tallow. I am especially interested in long-term, well-designed studies that track both responses of the encroaching shrubs and the broader ecosystem.

#Grassland restoration
Research about grassland restoration that is relevant to Texas ecosystems and broader theory behind grassland plant community assembly. Restoration approaches can include seeding, planting, microbial inoculation, invasive species removal, prescribed fire, grazing, mowing, haying, and manipulations of soil nutrients like carbon, nitrogen, and phosphorus. Interactions between grassland restoration activities and climate change are useful. I am especially interested in long-term studies and studies that include tests of the mechanisms that explain the outcomes.

#Invasive grasses relevant to Texas
Research about problematic non-native grasses found in Texas, including Eragrostis, Bothriochloa and Dichanthium species, Guineagrass, and Johnsongrass. I am interested in management approaches (chemical, mechanical, fire), especially studies that track long-term outcomes, as well as research about natural and biological controls (like pasture mealybug or competition with native grasses).

#Using drones/UAVs/UAS for ecological monitoring
Papers that develop or describe methods using drones for ecological monitoring, including LiDAR and multi-spectral imagery. I am interested in tracking woody plant biomass, mapping individual species or species groups (native and non-native), mapping shading by woody plants of cacti and herbaceous plants, tracking prescribed fire severity/outcomes, finding cryptic marsh birds, distinguishing between living and dead trees, and other topics related to land management of Texas ecosystems.
